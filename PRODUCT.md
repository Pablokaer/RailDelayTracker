# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Rail passengers, rail enthusiasts, operations-minded commuters, and analysts who need to understand live Irish Rail service conditions and historical punctuality. They use the product to scan current performance, compare Connolly and Heuston operations, inspect delays, plan station-to-station journeys, and locate live trains without losing the context behind the numbers.

## Product Purpose

IERailMetrics turns the public Irish Rail realtime feed and its accumulated history into a trustworthy operational dashboard. Success means that live status, network trends, station performance, route risk, journey options, and train positions remain easy to interpret while all data continues to come from the existing APIs, models, calculations, and persistence layer.

## Brand Personality

Operational, assured, and precise. The interface should feel like a professional rail control and analytics product: calm under pressure, information-dense without becoming noisy, and recognisably Irish through the established green palette and wolfhound mark.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS landing-page styling, decorative glass effects, oversized vanity metrics, playful consumer-travel visuals, invented data, icon or emoji substitutes for the official logo, and presentation choices that hide operational detail. Do not trade real functionality or data density for mockup fidelity.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve the truth of the data and make provenance, freshness, scope, and status obvious.
2. Support scanning first, then progressive inspection of routes, trains, stations, and trends.
3. Use one consistent operational vocabulary across dashboards, tables, filters, charts, and maps.
4. Make severity legible through text, shape, and contrast, never colour alone.
5. Keep every existing workflow and integration intact while improving hierarchy and responsiveness.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, keyboard navigation, focus visibility, semantic structure, reduced-motion preferences, and status communication that does not depend on colour alone. Dense tables and maps must remain usable on smaller screens through deliberate responsive alternatives.
